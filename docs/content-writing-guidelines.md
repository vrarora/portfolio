# Content writing guidelines

Use these rules for future portfolio copy and other content edits.

## Write plainly

- Lead with the useful point. Skip preambles, self-commentary, and summaries that repeat the point.
- Prefer concrete facts, specific nouns, and simple verbs. Use only information supported by the source material.
- Keep each paragraph focused. Remove repeated claims, filler, and details that do not help the reader.
- Match the length to the page and the reader's attention. Put optional detail behind an expansion when the page supports it.
- Describe the subject directly. Avoid promotional claims, inflated stakes, and vague attributions.
- Use the author's first-person voice for personal copy. Do not switch to "we" unless the author speaks for a group.

## Avoid common generated-writing patterns

- Avoid negative parallelism such as "not X, but Y" and "not only X, but Y" when a direct statement works.
- Use em dashes sparingly. Prefer punctuation that makes the sentence easy to follow.
- Avoid rhetorical questions followed by their own answers, artificial fragments, and announced conclusions.
- Do not force lists of three, repeat the same sentence opening, or disguise lists as successive prose paragraphs.
- Avoid invented labels, forced metaphors, quotable filler, and ornate words where plain ones work.
- Avoid canned transitions and overused terms such as "delve," "leverage," "robust," "quietly," and "it's worth noting."
- Do not add a trailing participial phrase merely to claim significance.
- Use straight quotes and ordinary punctuation. Use headings in sentence case.

These are editing checks, not a ban on every listed construction. Keep a pattern when accuracy, the author's voice, or clear meaning calls for it.
